01

Hello, MPC

Switch on, listen, look around, and save something that’s yours.

TimeAbout 15 to 20 minutes, plus Go Further
You’ll needThe MPC, charged, and headphones or monitors
OutcomeYour first saved project, HELLO1

The IdeaA sampler with a memory

An MPC is two instruments in one box. The sampler half holds recordings of sounds and plays them when you hit a pad. The sequencer half remembers what you played and plays it back for you, round and round, so you can listen, add another layer, and listen again.

Tonight is an introductory exploration; your own original track begins in Lesson 2. The MPC starts up with a demo project already loaded, so you’ll explore that: play its sequences, play its sounds by hand, swap one sound for another, and save the result under your own name. That last part is the important one. Saving is what lets you experiment freely for the rest of the book.

Five words worth knowing

Five words come up again and again:

  • Sample: one recorded sound.
  • Pad: one of the 16 rubber squares. In Sample Mode, each holds one sample.
  • Bank: a set of 16 pads. There are eight, lettered A to H.
  • Sequence: a recording of when pads were played, which loops.
  • Project: everything together, saved under one name.

The same physical pad has different jobs depending on the mode. Press [[SAMPLE]] and pad 1 plays the sound on pad 1. Press [[SEQ]] and pad 1 chooses sequence 1 instead. The pad hasn’t changed; the question it answers has. When something surprises you, the first thing to check is which mode button is lit.

Let’s Make SomethingYour first saved project

ASwitch on and listen

  1. Turn MAIN VOLUME fully down. Plug headphones into PHONES, or connect your monitors.
  2. Press POWER. After a moment the MPC loads its demo project automatically.
  3. Press [[PLAY]], then slowly raise MAIN VOLUME until it’s comfortable. The pads light up as the demo plays them.
  4. Press [[STOP]].

BLaunch some sequences

  1. Press [[SEQ]]. The pads now stand for sequences. Bright green pads have music recorded in them; dim pads are empty.
  2. Tap [[#1]] and press [[PLAY]].
  3. While it plays, tap [[#2]]. It flashes, waiting politely, then takes over when sequence 1 reaches its end. Try a few more lit pads in different orders.
  4. Press [[STOP]].
You just arranged musicChoosing which sequence plays next is the heart of arranging a track. You’ll come back to exactly this in Lesson 7.

CPlay the sounds yourself

  1. Press [[SAMPLE]]. Now each pad plays a single sound. Tap them all, softly and firmly: the pads respond to how hard you play.
  2. Watch the screen. It shows the shape of the last sound you played. This shape is called a waveform, and you’ll learn to read it in Lesson 5.

DSwap one sound for another

  1. Tap [[#2]] so it’s the selected pad. (Akai’s own tutorial uses this pad, as it holds a snare drum in the demo.)
  2. Press [[SAMPLE SELECT]]. The browser opens, showing folders of sounds grouped by type.
  3. Turn the ENCODER to the folder called Snares and press the ENCODER to open it.
  4. Turn the ENCODER slowly. Each sound plays as you land on it. (If nothing plays, press [[B3]], which switches previewing on and off.)
  5. When you hear a snare you like, press the ENCODER. It’s now on pad 2.
  6. Press [[SAMPLE SELECT]] to close the browser, then [[SEQ]], [[#1]] and [[PLAY]] to hear the demo with your snare in it. Press [[STOP]].

ESave it under your own name

  1. Hold [[SHIFT]] and press [[#16]] (PROJECT). The Project menu opens.
  2. Turn the ENCODER to [[~Save Project]] and press the ENCODER.
  3. Now type a name. Turn the ENCODER to choose a letter, then press the ENCODER to accept it and move to the next space. Spell HELLO1.

    Fixing typos: [[−]] and [[+]] move between letters, [[B2]] deletes one letter, [[SHIFT]] + [[B2]] clears the whole name. Hold [[SHIFT]] while choosing for capital letters.

  4. Press [[B3]] (Do It!). Saved.

FProve it’s safe

A quick confidence check: load your project back and hear that it’s really there.

  1. Hold [[SHIFT]] and press [[#16]] (PROJECT). Turn the ENCODER to [[~Load Project]] and press it.
  2. Choose [[~User]], the folder for projects you’ve saved, and press the ENCODER. Turn to HELLO1 and press the ENCODER to load it. If the MPC asks about unsaved changes, press [[B3]].
  3. Press [[SEQ]], tap [[#1]] and press [[PLAY]]. Your snare is there. Press [[STOP]].
How saving works on the MPC SampleKeep three things apart. The working project is what’s on the MPC right now; Akai says your progress is saved in the background, so switching off and on brings it back. Save Project makes a separate, named copy, sounds and sequences together, that you can load again later. Save Sample (Lesson 5) keeps one sound on its own for other projects. Starting a new project or loading another replaces the working project, and anything that isn’t in a named copy goes with it, which is why the MPC asks first. So this book saves a new named copy at the end of every lesson, like pages in a sketchbook.

Go FurtherA one-minute live set

About 10 to 15 minutes. Using HELLO1, perform the demo as if it were your own music.

  • Plan an order. Listen to the lit sequences and pick four. Write the order down: something calm to start, something fuller, a pause, a return.
  • Perform it. Press [[SEQ]] and queue your four sequences live while one plays, as in step 7.
  • Take something away. During your fullest sequence, press [[SAMPLE]], then [[MUTE]], and tap one pad to silence it (red pads are silent, yellow pads play). Tap it again to bring it back. Press [[MUTE]] to finish; [[SHIFT]] + [[MUTE]] unmutes everything. Nothing is deleted.
  • Add a moment. While a sequence plays, press [[PAD FX]] and hold a pad for a bar. Press harder for more effect. Press [[SEQ]] to put the pads back.
  • Hear another demo. [[SHIFT]] + [[#16]], [[~Load Project]], [[~Demos]]. HELLO1 stays safe under [[~User]].

What You’ve Learned

  • A sample is a sound on a pad; a sequence is a recording of pad hits.
  • [[SAMPLE]] and [[SEQ]] change what the pads do.
  • Sequences can be queued live, one after another.
  • [[SAMPLE SELECT]] swaps the sound on a pad.
  • [[SHIFT]] + [[#16]] saves a named copy, and [[~Load Project]] brings it back.

Lesson
02

Four Beats and a Heartbeat

Tempo, beats and bars, and a drum loop built from nothing.

TimeAbout 20 to 25 minutes, plus Go Further
Starts fromA brand new, empty project
OutcomeYour first original drum loop, GENTLY2

The IdeaCounting to four

Tap your foot along to almost any song and you’re tapping the beat: a steady pulse underneath the music. How fast that pulse goes is the tempo, measured in BPM, beats per minute. At 60 BPM there’s one beat every second, like a clock. At 120, two. We’ll use 84 BPM, an unhurried walking pace that suits atmospheric music.

Beats are grouped into bars. This project uses four beats per bar, the most common pattern in electronic music. Musicians count bars out loud as “one, two, three, four, one, two, three, four”, leaning on the “one” because it marks the start of each bar.

A drum loop gives each drum a job in that count:

  • The kick is the low thump. It plays on beats 1 and 3, like a slow heartbeat.
  • The snare is the sharper crack. It answers on beats 2 and 4, which musicians call the backbeat.
  • The hi-hat is the light tick that keeps time in between, here on every half-beat. Half-beats are counted “and”: one and two and three… In the diagrams, “and” is written +.

Jamming and recording

Tapping pads in Sample Mode is jamming: you hear the sounds, and nothing is kept. Recording is different: the MPC writes down when you tapped each pad, so it can play your rhythm back for you. It records timing, not audio, which is why you can change a drum sound later and the rhythm stays the same.

Recording always goes into the selected sequence, so the order matters: choose the sequence and set it up first, then arm recording, then play.

Overdubbing and quantising

When you record a sequence, the MPC loops round and round. When it reaches the end, it carries on recording in overdub, adding whatever you play on top of what’s already there. Building up layers like this means you only ever have to play one drum at a time.

Nobody hits pads perfectly in time, and that’s fine. Quantising gently moves each hit to the nearest line of an invisible grid. The spacing of that grid is called Q. With Q at 1/16, a bar is divided into sixteen tiny slots, fine enough to keep your feel but tidy up the wobbles. Quantising only moves hits in time. It doesn’t change the tempo, and it can’t fix a hit that landed on the wrong beat: that one you simply play again.

Let’s Make SomethingA drum loop of your own

AStart a fresh project

  1. Hold [[SHIFT]] and press [[#16]] (PROJECT). Turn the ENCODER to [[~New Project]] and press it.
  2. If the MPC warns about unsaved changes, press [[B3]] to carry on. HELLO1 is safely saved. Everything is now clear: no sounds, no sequences.

BChoose three drums

  1. Press [[SAMPLE]]. Check the bank letter beside MAIN VOLUME shows A. (If not, press [[PAD BANK]] until it does.) From now on, pad 1 in bank A is written [[#A1]].
  2. Tap [[#A1]] to select it, then press [[SAMPLE SELECT]].
  3. The folders are named by type of sound, like the Snares folder you met in Lesson 1. Find the folder of kick drums, open it with the ENCODER and listen through. For atmospheric music, choose a soft, round kick rather than a clicky one. Press the ENCODER to load it, then [[SAMPLE SELECT]] to close.
  4. Do the same for [[#A2]] with a snare or clap. Gentle ones suit this music: look for a rimshot, a soft clap, or a snare with a bit of room around it.
  5. Do the same for [[#A3]] with a closed hi-hat: a short, crisp tick.
  6. Play pads A1, A2 and A3 together for a moment. That’s your drum kit.

CSet up the sequence

  1. Press [[SEQ]] and tap [[#1]] (bank A should still be lit). It flashes to show sequence 1 is selected. Everything you set next belongs to this sequence.
  2. Hold [[B1]] and turn the ENCODER until the tempo reads 84. Let go of B1. Then press [[B2]] until the screen shows GBL, so every sequence in this project shares the same tempo.
  3. Turn [[@K1]] until the length reads 2 bars.
  4. Turn [[@K2]] until Q reads 1/16.
  5. Press [[B3]] so Record Quantisation is on (the screen shows its state).
  6. Hold [[SHIFT]] and press [[TAP TEMPO]] until the metronome setting on screen reads Record: the click plays while you record, then politely stops. [[SHIFT]] + [[@K3]] sets how loud the click is.
  7. Hold [[SHIFT]] and press [[#4]] (COUNT-IN) so the 1234 icon turns red.

DRecord, one drum per time round

  1. Press [[SEQ RECORD]]. It flashes: recording is armed, and the pads play sounds again, ready to be recorded.
  2. Press [[PLAY]]. You’ll hear one bar of clicks counting you in. Then recording begins: tap [[#A1]] (kick) on one and three, counting quietly along with the click.
  3. Keep going. Each time round the loop you hear what you’ve already played. On the next pass, tap [[#A2]] (snare) on two and four.
  4. For the hi-hats, press [[NOTE REPEAT]] so it lights. The screen shows note lengths above [[B1]] [[B2]] [[B3]]; press the one for 1/8 (Akai’s guide doesn’t list the choices, so if 1/8 isn’t there, pick the nearest). Now hold [[#A3]] down for one full time round the loop and let go. Press [[NOTE REPEAT]] again to turn it off.
  5. Press [[SEQ RECORD]] to stop recording while the loop keeps playing. Listen. Then press [[STOP]]. (Recording is now off, and Record Quantisation, the metronome and the count-in stay set for the lessons ahead.)
If something went wrong A messy pass? [[SHIFT]] + [[−]] (UNDO) removes everything recorded since you pressed PLAY, so you can try again.
Want to redo just one drum? Press [[SEQ]], light bank A and tap [[#1]]. Press [[SEQ RECORD]] so it flashes, hold [[ERASE]] and tap that drum’s pad. All its hits disappear from the sequence; the others stay. Press [[STOP]], then record that drum again from step 16.
Forgot to switch quantising on? Hold [[SHIFT]] and press [[#14]] (TIME CORRECT). Press [[B2]] to choose all pads, set [[@K1]] to 1/16 and press [[B3]] (Do It!).

ESave it

  1. [[SHIFT]] + [[#16]], then [[~Save Project]]. Name it GENTLY2 and press [[B3]] (Do It!).
Which sequence is for whatYour track uses sequences 1 to 5 (from Lesson 7), and Lesson 8 puts a backup in the next free one, normally 6. 9 to 14 are your sketchpad: Lesson 2’s Go Further uses 9 to 11, Lesson 4’s uses 12 to 14. 15 is a temporary helper in Lesson 5, and 16 is kept for Lesson 7’s Go Further. Nothing in a sketchpad sequence plays in your song unless you put it there. Before using any of them, check its pad is dim in Sequence Mode.

Go FurtherThree ways to feel the same beat

About 15 minutes. Make three variations of your loop and decide which feel you like best.

  • Make three copies. Press [[SEQ]], light bank A, and check pads 9 to 11 are dim. Tap [[#1]], hold [[SHIFT]] + [[ERASE]] (COPY), tap [[#9]], [[#10]] and [[#11]] so they turn red, then let go.
  • 9, a skipped heartbeat: press [[SEQ]], light bank A and tap [[#9]]. Press [[SEQ RECORD]], then [[PLAY]], and add one extra kick ([[#A1]]) on the “and” after four. Press [[SEQ RECORD]], then [[STOP]].
  • 10, half-time: press [[SEQ]], light bank A and tap [[#10]]. Press [[SEQ RECORD]]; don’t press PLAY yet. With bank A lit, hold [[ERASE]] and tap [[#A2]] to clear the snare. Press [[PLAY]] and play the snare on three only. Slow and heavy. Press [[SEQ RECORD]], then [[STOP]].
  • 11, your idea: press [[SEQ]], light bank A and tap [[#11]]. Press [[SEQ RECORD]]; don’t press PLAY yet. With bank A lit, hold [[ERASE]] and tap [[#A3]] to clear the hats. Press [[PLAY]] and play them your own way: only on the beats, or faster with [[NOTE REPEAT]] (press it again to switch it off). Press [[SEQ RECORD]], then [[STOP]].
  • Compare. Queue 1, 9, 10 and 11 one after another. Which feels calmest? Which makes you nod? Try [[@K3]] (Swing) on one of the copies.

Sequence 1 stays as it is. Save as GENTLY2B (saving over an existing name isn’t described in Akai’s guide, so this book always uses a new one), and start Lesson 3 from whichever copy you like.

What You’ve Learned

  • BPM is speed; here a bar is four beats.
  • Kick on 1 and 3, snare on 2 and 4, hats on every half-beat.
  • [[SEQ RECORD]] then [[PLAY]]; passes overdub on top of each other.
  • Recording keeps timing, not sound; quantising tidies timing, not tempo.
  • Undo, erase-a-pad and Time Correct fix mistakes.

Your CreationGENTLY2

A two-bar drum loop at 84 BPM in sequence 1, with your own kick, snare and hats on pads A1 to A3. This is the heartbeat of your track.
