Getting Back to a Known State

When the MPC is doing something you don’t understand, don’t hunt for the cause. Work down this list. Steps 1 to 8 stop, disarm, leave temporary modes and clear mutes: none of them changes a sound or a sequence. Step 9 is different, so stop before it if you can.

  1. Stop. Press [[STOP]]. Press it twice quickly to silence every sound that’s still ringing.
  2. Disarm recording. [[SEQ RECORD]] should be dark, not flashing. If it’s flashing, press [[STOP]]; if it still flashes, press [[SEQ RECORD]] once.
  3. Return to Sample Mode. Press [[SAMPLE]] so it’s lit. If a menu stays on the screen, look for Cancel or Back above [[B1]].
  4. Leave Chop Mode. If [[CHOP]] is lit, press it.
  5. Check the bank. Look at the letter beside MAIN VOLUME. Drums are in A, bass in B, your shaped sounds in C, recordings in D. Press [[PAD BANK]] to change.
  6. Clear mutes. If [[MUTE]] is lit, press it. Then [[SHIFT]] + [[MUTE]] (UNMUTE ALL). Muting never deletes anything.
  7. Disengage the special buttons. [[KNOB FX]], [[NOTE REPEAT]] and [[16 LEVELS]] should be dark. Press any that are lit.
  8. Choose your loop. Press [[SEQ]], check bank A is lit, tap [[#1]] and press [[PLAY]]. If it sounds right, you’re back.
  9. Still wrong? Go back to a saved copy. This one can lose work. Loading replaces the working project, and everything since that copy was saved is gone. If what’s there might be worth keeping, save it first under a new name. Then [[SHIFT]] + [[#16]], [[~Load Project]], [[~User]], and choose your most recent copy.
This is not a factory resetA factory reset is far more drastic. It lives in the MIDI Config menu ([[SHIFT]] + [[#8]]), which offers two options:
  • Reset Factory Settings resets every setting and restarts the MPC.
  • Reset Factory Data deletes all your projects and samples on the internal drive, and can’t be undone.
This book never needs either. If you’re ever told to try one, back up to a microSD card first.

Your Sounds and Storage

Everything in this book lives on the MPC’s internal drive: 8 GB, about 2 GB of which holds the factory sounds and demos. A microSD card is optional extra storage, and it matters for one reason: it’s the only storage Akai describes a computer reaching.

Factory samples
The built-in sounds, in folders such as Snares. Browse them with [[SAMPLE SELECT]]. Loading one puts it on the selected pad in your project.
User samples
Sounds you’ve saved with SAVE SAMPLE ([[SHIFT]] + [[SAMPLE SELECT]]). They go into a folder called MPC-Sample/Samples, on whichever drive you choose with [[B2]], ready to load into any project.
Sample packs
Sounds downloaded on a computer. They must go on a microSD card (see below), then appear in the browser when you press [[B2]] to switch to the external drive.
Projects
Your sounds, sequences and song, saved together with SAVE PROJECT. Saving copies the project’s files to a new location, so a saved project carries its own sounds with it. Projects on the internal drive appear under [[~Load Project]], [[~User]].
Kits
Ready-made drum kits, under [[~Load Project]], [[~Kits]]. The ENCODER loads a kit with four default sequences; [[B3]] (Load Kit) loads only its samples. Akai doesn’t say what happens to your current sequences, so save first.
Background saving
Akai says progress is saved in the background, so switching off and on brings back the working project. It isn’t a named copy: a new or loaded project replaces it.
Exported audio
A song exported as an audio mixdown (Lesson 8): a finished file for listening and sharing. Akai’s guide doesn’t say which drive it’s saved to.

Which to useInternal or microSD?

Use the internal drive for your projects and everyday work: Akai says Load Project’s User folder holds projects saved there. Use a microSD card for sample packs, for backups, and for anything you want to copy to or from a computer.

Optional, needs a computerCopying files with SD Card Access

  1. Push a microSD card fully into the slot on the left side and connect the MPC to the computer with the USB-C cable.
  2. [[SHIFT]] + [[#16]] (PROJECT). Choose [[~SD Card Access]] and press the ENCODER. After a few seconds the card appears on the computer as a drive. The MPC can’t do anything else while it’s in this mode.
  3. Copy files across. Leave the MPC Sample folder the MPC created alone, and put sample packs in a folder of your own.
  4. Eject the drive on the computer first, then press [[B1]] on the MPC to leave SD Card Access.

File types. Akai lists eight formats the MPC Sample can import, including WAV, AIFF, MP3 and FLAC, at 44.1, 48 or 96 kHz and 16 or 24 bit. For ordinary sample packs, WAV is the simplest choice: if a pack comes in several editions, pick WAV. One sample-pack publisher (not Akai) reports that packs need firmware 1.2.1 or later.

When Things Go Wrong

Most puzzles come down to one of four things: the wrong mode, the wrong bank, the knobs belonging to Knob FX, or recording still armed. If you’re in a hurry, start with Getting Back to a Known State. Otherwise, find your symptom below. If the screen ever disagrees with this book, believe the screen, and please report it at github.com/slowphase/mpc-gently.

Part oneSound and samples

A pad seems empty

Symptom
You tap a pad and hear nothing, and no waveform appears.
Cause
There’s no sample on that pad in this bank. Or you’re in Sequence Mode, where dim pads mean empty sequences, not missing sounds.
Fix
  1. Press [[SAMPLE]] so pads play sounds.
  2. Check the bank letter beside MAIN VOLUME; press [[PAD BANK]] to find the bank you meant.
  3. To look at a pad without playing it, hold [[SAMPLE]] and press the pad.
  4. If it really is empty, load a sound with [[SAMPLE SELECT]] (Lesson 1).
Prevention
Keep each kind of sound in its own bank, as this book does, and glance at the bank letter before you play.

A sample plays silently or too quietly

Symptom
The waveform is there, but the sound is faint or missing.
Cause
Usually one of: the pad is muted; its Mix volume is low; a slow Attack hides the start; Start and End sit around a quiet part; soft playing with high Velocity Sens; or MAIN VOLUME or Kit Volume is low.
Fix
  1. In Sample Mode, press [[SHIFT]] + [[MUTE]] (UNMUTE ALL).
  2. Press [[B1]] until Mix appears and raise [[@K1]] (Volume).
  3. Press [[B1]] until Amp Env appears and lower [[@K1]] (Attack).
  4. On the Trim page, check Start and End, then try Normalize ([[SHIFT]] + [[B2]]).
  5. For even volume however you play, try [[SHIFT]] + [[#1]] (FULL LEVEL).
Prevention
Change one setting at a time and tap the pad after each change, so you always know what made the difference.

Parts of my waveform are greyed out

Symptom
The pad plays, but on the Trim page some of the waveform, usually at the beginning or end, looks grey or dimmed.
Cause
Start or End has been moved. The part outside them is skipped in normal playback, but it’s still in the sample unless [[SHIFT]] + [[#13]] (TRIM SAMPLE) has cut it away.
Fix
  1. Select the pad and press [[B1]] until Trim appears.
  2. Turn [[@K1]] (Start) all the way down and [[@K2]] (End) all the way up. The whole sound plays again.
  3. If TRIM SAMPLE was used, the cut part is gone for good from this project; a saved copy from before the trim still has it.
Prevention
Lesson 5’s “Five ways to cut a sound” table shows which cuts can be undone. Only TRIM SAMPLE is permanent.

My entire waveform is grey and the pad is silent

Symptom
The whole picture is grey, and tapping the pad plays nothing.
Cause
Akai’s guide says a muted sample’s waveform appears greyed out, and the sample won’t trigger. Muting changes nothing in the sound itself.
Fix
  1. Press [[SAMPLE]], then [[MUTE]].
  2. Tap the pad so it turns yellow (unmuted). Red means muted.
  3. Press [[MUTE]] again to finish, or press [[SHIFT]] + [[MUTE]] to unmute everything.
Prevention
After using MUTE in a performance, unmute everything before you start editing.

A chopped sample doesn’t behave

Symptom
Too many slices, too few, or the slices vanish when you leave Chop Mode.
Cause
Threshold chopping places slices where it hears new sounds, so the Threshold setting decides how many. The slices play from the pads only while Chop Mode is on; to give a slice a pad of its own, you Extract it.
Fix
  1. In Chop Mode, hold [[SHIFT]] and turn [[@K3]]: higher means fewer slices.
  2. For equal slices regardless of content, set [[@K3]] to Regions 8.
  3. To keep a slice, tap it and press [[SHIFT]] + [[B1]] (Extract). It lands on the next free pad (Lesson 6).
Prevention
Leave a second of silence between sounds when you record, and trim the ends first. Note that editing slices by hand switches Chop Type to Manual, and slice edits can’t be undone.

A sound keeps playing

Symptom
A sound rings on, or loops, after you’ve stopped playing.
Cause
LOOP is on for that pad; a long sound with a long Decay is overlapping itself in Poly; or One Shot is playing a long sample right to its end.
Fix
  1. Press [[STOP]] twice quickly to silence everything.
  2. Select the pad. If [[LOOP]] is lit, press it.
  3. Shorten Decay on the Amp Env page, or move End in on the Trim page.
  4. To let your hand decide, switch to Note On ([[SHIFT]] + [[CHOP]]): the sound stops when you let go (Lesson 3).
Prevention
Use Mono or a Mute Group for sounds that shouldn’t overlap, as you did for the bass.

A sample plays at the wrong speed or pitch

Symptom
A sound is suddenly higher, lower, faster or slower than you expect.
Cause
Semi Tune or Fine Tune has moved; Warp is set (and at Seq it follows the sequence tempo); 16 Levels is still on, so each pad plays a different pitch; or the fader is set to control tuning.
Fix
  1. Press [[B2]] until Tune appears. Set Semi Tune to 0 and [[@K3]] (Warp) to Off.
  2. If [[16 LEVELS]] is lit, press it to switch it off.
  3. Check the fader’s job with [[SHIFT]] + [[#9]] (FADER).
Prevention
Before copying a pad, make sure the original is the version you want: copies keep its tuning.

The fader or knobs don’t respond

Symptom
You turn K1 to K3 or move the fader and nothing changes.
Cause
Knob FX is engaged, so K1 to K3 belong to the effect; or the control is waiting to catch up with the value on screen; or the fader controls something you’re not listening for, on the selected pad only.
Fix
  1. If [[KNOB FX]] is lit, press it to disengage.
  2. Keep turning towards the value shown on screen. The fader picks up once it passes the current value.
  3. Check the fader’s job with [[SHIFT]] + [[#9]], and tap the pad you mean to change.
  4. To change how controls take over, look in [[SHIFT]] + [[#8]] (MIDI CONFIG): Instant, Pickup or Scaled.
Prevention
Before editing sample settings, check KNOB FX. If it is lit, press it to disengage it.

An effect doesn’t seem to work

Symptom
You turn the Knob FX controls, or press Pad FX, and hear no change.
Cause
Knob FX only affects pads that are lit in FX SELECT; it may be bypassed; its Mix may be low; Pad FX only works while you hold a pad in PAD FX mode, with something playing; the Compressor may be switched off.
Fix
  1. Open FX SELECT ([[SHIFT]] + [[KNOB FX]]) and check the pads you’re playing are bright, and that the effect isn’t bypassed ([[B3]] is the Bypass).
  2. Engage [[KNOB FX]] and raise [[@K3]] (Mix).
  3. For Pad FX, press [[PLAY]] first, then [[PAD FX]], and hold a pad.
  4. For the Compressor, open [[SHIFT]] + [[#5]] and check [[B3]] has switched it on.
Prevention
Keep Lesson 3’s three ideas apart: choosing the effect and pads, handing the knobs to it ([[KNOB FX]]), and bypassing it ([[B3]] in FX SELECT).

Part twoSequences and recording

You recorded into the wrong sequence

Symptom
Your new part is in sequence 2, but you meant sequence 1.
Cause
Recording always goes into the selected (flashing) sequence, whichever that was.
Fix
  1. Straight away, press [[SHIFT]] + [[−]] (UNDO) to remove the take.
  2. Press [[SEQ]], check bank A, tap the sequence you meant, and record again.
  3. If the wrong sequence was empty and you’d like to keep the take, copy it instead: select it, [[SHIFT]] + [[ERASE]] (COPY), and tap a dim (empty) sequence. Copying onto a sequence that already has hits isn’t described in Akai’s guide.
Prevention
Follow the order in Lesson 2’s workflow diagram: choose and set up the sequence first, then arm, then play.

Recording doesn’t seem to start

Symptom
You play the pads, but nothing is kept.
Cause
[[SEQ RECORD]] wasn’t armed (flashing) before [[PLAY]]; the count-in bar hasn’t finished; or, for sound recording, the Threshold is higher than your sound.
Fix
  1. Press [[STOP]]. Press [[SEQ RECORD]] so it flashes, then [[PLAY]].
  2. If the 1234 icon is red, wait for one bar of clicks before playing.
  3. Played something good without recording? [[SHIFT]] + [[SEQ RECORD]] (RECALL) puts the last loop you played into the sequence.
  4. For sound recording, turn [[@K3]] (Threshold) down and check the Source (Lesson 6).
Prevention
Watch for the flashing SEQ RECORD button before you start playing.

Pads launch sequences instead of sounds

Symptom
You tap a pad for a drum and a sequence starts instead.
Cause
You’re in Sequence Mode, where pads choose sequences.
Fix
  1. Press [[SAMPLE]].
  2. Or, to record, press [[SEQ RECORD]]: once armed, the pads play sounds.
Prevention
Check which mode button is lit before you play (Lesson 1, “Five words”).

A recording changed a pattern you liked

Symptom
Extra notes have appeared in a finished loop, or a part has gone.
Cause
Recording overdubs: every pass adds what you play. Erasing removes a whole pad’s hits from the selected sequence.
Fix
  1. Straight away: [[SHIFT]] + [[−]] (UNDO) removes everything recorded since recording began.
  2. Extra hits from one pad only: arm [[SEQ RECORD]], hold [[ERASE]] and tap that pad. Then record it again.
  3. Erased a whole sequence? Try [[SHIFT]] + [[−]]; Akai’s guide doesn’t say whether Undo restores an erased sequence.
  4. Otherwise, [[SHIFT]] + [[#16]], [[~Load Project]], [[~User]], and choose your most recent copy.
Prevention
Before experimenting on a loop you love, copy it into a dim sketchpad sequence (9 to 14), and save a new project copy at each milestone.

You want to remove one drum, not everything

Symptom
The hats are wrong, but the kick and snare are perfect.
Cause
Erasing works pad by pad, inside the selected sequence only.
Fix
  1. Press [[SEQ]], light bank A and tap the sequence. Press [[SEQ RECORD]] so it flashes; you don’t need to press PLAY.
  2. Check the right bank is lit, hold [[ERASE]] and tap the drum’s pad. Every hit from that pad goes from this sequence; the sound stays on the pad. Press [[STOP]].
  3. Just one hit? Use [[SHIFT]] + [[SEQ]] (STEP EDIT): turn the ENCODER to the step, tap the pad to select its hit, and press [[B3]] to delete it.
Prevention
Record one drum per pass, as in Lesson 2. Only erase with [[SEQ RECORD]] flashing: in Sample Mode, stopped and not armed, [[ERASE]] and a pad erases the sound from the pad.

The loop sounds out of time

Symptom
Hits feel early, late or uneven.
Cause
Record Quantisation was off; Swing is turned up; a hit landed on the wrong beat (quantising moves hits to the nearest grid line, which may be the wrong one); or the sequence length doesn’t match what you played.
Fix
  1. Tidy what’s there: [[SHIFT]] + [[#14]] (TIME CORRECT), [[B2]] for all pads, [[@K1]] to 1/16, [[B3]] (Do It!).
  2. In Sequence Mode, check [[@K3]] (Swing).
  3. For a hit on the wrong beat, erase that pad and play it again with the metronome on.
  4. Check [[@K1]] (Length) shows the number of bars you meant.
Prevention
Switch Record Quantisation on ([[B3]] in Sequence Mode) and use the metronome and count-in, as set up in Lesson 2.

You want to jam without recording

Symptom
You’d like to play along with your loop without adding anything to it.
Cause
Pads only record while [[SEQ RECORD]] is armed.
Fix
  1. Make sure [[SEQ RECORD]] is dark. If it’s flashing, press [[STOP]] (and [[SEQ RECORD]] once if it still flashes).
  2. Press [[SEQ]], choose a sequence, press [[PLAY]], then press [[SAMPLE]] and play the pads freely.
  3. Played something worth keeping? [[SHIFT]] + [[SEQ RECORD]] (RECALL) adds the last loop you played to the sequence.
Prevention
Jam first, record second. RECALL is a safety net, not a plan.

A sequence plays, but some sounds are missing

Symptom
The loop runs, but a drum or the bass is silent.
Cause
The pad is muted; its hits were erased from this sequence (each sequence has its own hits); the sound on the pad was erased or replaced; or its volume is down.
Fix
  1. In Sample Mode, press [[SHIFT]] + [[MUTE]] (UNMUTE ALL).
  2. Tap the pad: if it’s empty, the sound was removed; reload it, or go back to a saved copy.
  3. Compare with another sequence: if the part plays there, it was erased from this one. Record it again (Lesson 7, “Same sounds, different notes”).
Prevention
Erase pads only with the right sequence selected, and check the bank letter first.

Part threeProjects, storage and recovery

A recording seems to have disappeared

Symptom
You recorded a sound, and now you can’t find it.
Cause
It’s on a different pad or bank (Extract and RECALL use the next free pad); or you started a new project or loaded another before saving.
Fix
  1. Press [[PAD BANK]] through the banks, tapping pads near where you recorded.
  2. If it happened moments ago, open [[SAMPLE RECORD]] and press [[SHIFT]] + [[SAMPLE RECORD]] (RECALL): the MPC keeps the last 25 seconds of its input.
  3. If the project was replaced, [[SHIFT]] + [[#16]], [[~Load Project]], [[~User]], and choose your most recent copy.
Prevention
Save a new project copy as soon as you’ve recorded something you love.

A project wasn’t saved as expected

Symptom
Your saved project isn’t in the list, or it’s missing recent work.
Cause
The save page was left with [[B1]] (Cancel) instead of [[B3]] (Do It!); work happened after the save; or the project was saved to the microSD card, while [[~Load Project]], [[~User]] lists projects on the internal drive.
Fix
  1. Open [[~Load Project]], [[~User]] and scroll the whole list.
  2. If the name is there, load it: anything after that save will need redoing.
  3. If you saved to the card, put it back in. Whether Load Project can open projects from the card isn’t described in Akai’s guide.
Prevention
Always finish a save with [[B3]] and wait for it to complete. Save to the internal drive, and use the card for backups.

A sample was recorded but not saved separately

Symptom
You recorded a sound, but never used SAVE SAMPLE. Is it safe?
Cause
A recording lives in your project. Saving the project keeps it; SAVE SAMPLE is only for using the sound in other projects.
Fix
  1. Save the project ([[SHIFT]] + [[#16]], [[~Save Project]]). Your recording is kept with it.
  2. If you want the sound elsewhere too, select its pad and press [[SHIFT]] + [[SAMPLE SELECT]] (SAVE SAMPLE).
Prevention
Before starting a new project, save the current one. Unsaved work is lost when you start or load another.

You can’t find your saved project

Symptom
You saved a project but don’t see it when loading.
Cause
You’re looking in [[~Demos]] or [[~Kits]] instead of [[~User]], or it was saved under a different name or to the microSD card.
Fix
  1. [[SHIFT]] + [[#16]], [[~Load Project]], [[~User]], then turn the ENCODER slowly through the whole list.
Prevention
Use one naming pattern (GENTLY2, GENTLY3, …) so your projects sort together.

You can’t find a downloaded sample pack

Symptom
You downloaded sounds on a computer, and the MPC can’t see them.
Cause
Downloads live on the computer until you copy them to a microSD card. Akai describes computer access only for the card, not the internal drive.
Fix
  1. Copy the pack to a microSD card using SD Card Access (see Your Sounds and Storage).
  2. On the MPC, press [[SAMPLE SELECT]], then [[B2]] to switch to the external drive, and browse to the pack’s folder.
  3. Check the files are a type the MPC imports (WAV is safest) and the pack’s notes for its firmware version.
Prevention
Keep packs in a folder of your own on the card, apart from the MPC Sample folder.

You want an earlier version back

Symptom
Today’s experiments went wrong, and you want yesterday’s track.
Cause
Every lesson saved a new copy, so each earlier version still exists.
Fix
  1. [[SHIFT]] + [[#16]], [[~Load Project]], [[~User]].
  2. Choose the copy you want, for example GENTLY6, and press the ENCODER. If asked about unsaved changes, [[B3]] carries on and loses them; to keep them, back out and save a new copy first.
  3. Carry on from there, and save under a new name when you’re done.
Prevention
Never reach for a factory reset to undo a mistake: it deletes your saved work (see Getting Back to a Known State).

Glossary

Arm
To get recording ready. When [[SEQ RECORD]] flashes, recording is armed; it starts when you press PLAY.
Automation
Knob or fader moves recorded into a sequence, so a setting changes as it plays.
Attack
How long a sound takes to reach full volume. Slow attack means a gentle fade-in.
Bar
A group of beats. This book uses four beats per bar, counted “one, two, three, four”, a common pattern in electronic music.
Bass
The lowest part of the music, giving it weight.
Beat
The steady pulse you’d tap your foot to.
BPM
Beats per minute: the tempo, or speed, of the music.
Chop
To cut a recording into slices, each playable from its own pad.
Compressor
An effect that turns down the loudest moments so the overall sound is steadier and fuller.
Count-in
A bar of clicks before recording starts, so you can find the beat.
Cutoff
The point where a filter starts removing sound.
Decay
How a sound fades away after it starts. Release is similar, for sounds that play only while a pad is held.
Envelope
A shape that changes something, usually volume or filter, over the life of a sound.
Extract
Copying one slice of a chopped sound to its own pad, leaving the original whole.
Filter
Removes some frequencies. Low-pass makes sound darker; high-pass makes it thinner.
Home note
The note a piece of music feels most at rest on. Also called the root.
Kit
The set of sounds on the pads of a project. Factory kits are under Load Project, Kits.
Knob FX
One effect applied to the pads you choose. The KNOB FX button hands K1 to K3 to it; [[B3]] in FX SELECT bypasses it.
Layering
Playing two or more sounds together to make a richer one.
Loop
A short piece of music that repeats. Also the LOOP button, which repeats a single sound.
Metronome
A click that marks the beat while you record.
Mono / Poly
Mono: a new hit stops the last. Poly: hits overlap and ring together.
Mute Group
Pads in the same group cut each other off, so only one plays at a time.
Note On / One Shot
Note On: a sound plays only while its pad is held. One Shot: a tap plays the whole sound.
Normalize
Raising a sound so its loudest point reaches full level.
Note Repeat
Makes a held pad repeat steadily at a chosen note length.
Octave
Twelve semitones. The same note, higher or lower.
Overdub
Recording new parts on top of what’s already recorded.
Pad bank
One of eight sets of 16 pads, lettered A to H.
Pan
Where a sound sits from left to right.
Pitch
How high or low a sound is.
Project
Everything you’re working on: sounds, sequences and song, saved together.
Quantise
Moving recorded hits to the nearest point on a timing grid. The grid spacing is called Q.
Resample
Recording what a sequence plays onto a new pad, as one new sound.
Resonance
A ringing emphasis at a filter’s cutoff point.
Reverb
An effect that imitates the echoes of a room or hall.
Sample
A recorded sound, ready to play from a pad.
Section
A part of a track, such as the intro, main section or breakdown.
Semitone
The smallest step between notes in Western music; one piano key.
Sequence
A recording of when pads were played, which loops.
Slice
One piece of a chopped recording.
Song
On the MPC Sample, a list of sequences played in order.
Swing
Delaying every other small note slightly, for a looser, rolling feel.
Time Stretch
A Warp mode that changes a sound’s length without changing its pitch.
Tempo
The speed of the music, in BPM.
Threshold
A level that triggers something: recording, or where chop slices fall.
Velocity
How hard a pad is hit, which usually sets how loud the sound plays.
Warp
Stretching a sound longer or shorter, with or without changing its pitch.
Waveform
A picture of a sound over time. Its height shows loudness; for regular, pitched sounds, how tightly its waves repeat shows pitch.

Where Next

You made a track.
Now make another, and listen for what you’d do differently.

The fastest way to grow from here isn’t learning more buttons. It’s making lots of small things. A few ideas for future evenings:

  • One loop a night. Ten minutes, one new project, one loop. Don’t judge it; save it and move on. After a month you’ll have a sketchbook of ideas.
  • Copy a groove you love. Pick a favourite atmospheric track and try to recreate just its drum pattern. You’ll learn more about rhythm than any book can teach.
  • A sound walk. Take the MPC around the house, or out into the garden at dusk, and record ten sounds. Build a track from nothing else.
  • Explore the corners. Flex Beat ([[SHIFT]] + [[PAD FX]]), Step Edit ([[SHIFT]] + [[SEQ]]), and the fader’s other jobs ([[SHIFT]] + [[#9]]) are all waiting.
  • Learn a scale. Copy one sound to seven pads and set their Semi Tune to 0, +2, +3, +5, +7, +8 and +10: the notes of a minor scale, a ready-made palette for melodies.
  • Watch and read. Akai’s MPC Sample videos on their YouTube channel and the full online User Guide go deeper on everything here.
