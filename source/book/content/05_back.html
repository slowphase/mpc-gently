When Things Go Wrong

Almost every MPC puzzle comes down to one of three things: the wrong mode, the wrong bank, or the knobs belonging to an effect. Check those first.

The pads start sequences instead of playing sounds.

You’re in Sequence Mode. Press [[SAMPLE]].

My sounds or sequences have disappeared.

Look at the bank letter beside MAIN VOLUME. You’re probably on another bank. Press [[PAD BANK]] until the right letter is lit. If a sequence really has gone, load your last saved copy: [[SHIFT]] + [[#16]], [[~Load Project]], [[~User]].

Turning K1 to K3 doesn’t change my sound.

If [[KNOB FX]] is lit, the knobs belong to the effect. Press [[KNOB FX]] to hand them back. Otherwise, the knob may need to “catch up”: keep turning towards the arrow on screen.

No sound at all.

Turn MAIN VOLUME up. Check your IEMs are fully pushed into PHONES. Check [[MUTE]] isn’t on and no pads are red; [[SHIFT]] + [[MUTE]] unmutes everything. Check the pad actually holds a sound (the screen shows a waveform).

Pressing PLAY gives silence or the wrong music.

In Sequence Mode, the flashing pad is the sequence that will play. Tap the one you want (with bank A lit) and press [[PLAY]].

My recording is a mess of extra notes.

Recording loops round and keeps adding. Press [[SHIFT]] + [[−]] to undo the whole take, or arm [[SEQ RECORD]], hold [[ERASE]] and press a pad to clear just that sound.

My timing sounds sloppy.

Switch on Record Quantisation ([[B3]] in Sequence Mode) before recording, or tidy afterwards with [[SHIFT]] + [[#14]] (TIME CORRECT). And check Swing ([[@K3]]) isn’t turned right up.

A sound cuts off early.

Check the Play page: Mono stops each hit when the next starts, and a Mute Group lets pads silence each other. Check Decay on Amp Env, and the End point on Trim.

The mix sounds harsh or distorted.

The meters are probably hitting the top. Lower Kit Volume ([[SHIFT]] + [[@K2]] on the Mix page) or individual pad volumes, and keep compressor Amount moderate.

Sample recording won’t start.

Check [[@K1]] Source is Mic, and lower [[@K3]] Threshold so quieter sounds can trigger it.

I can’t hear the bass.

Use your IEMs. The built-in speaker is too small for deep notes.

I started a New Project by mistake.

Anything you saved is safe. Load it from [[~Load Project]], [[~User]]. Work done since the last save is the only thing at risk, which is why each lesson ends with a save.

It won’t charge.

Use the supplied cable and a 5 V, 2 A charger. With some chargers, the MPC only charges while switched off.

The screen doesn’t match this book.

A firmware update may have changed something. Believe the screen, and check Akai’s online User Guide for the latest details.

Glossary

Attack
How long a sound takes to reach full volume. Slow attack means a gentle fade-in.
Bar
A group of beats. This book uses four beats per bar, counted “one, two, three, four”, a common pattern in electronic music.
Bass
The lowest part of the music, giving it weight.
Beat
The steady pulse you’d tap your foot to.
BPM
Beats per minute: the tempo, or speed, of the music.
Chop
To cut a recording into slices, each playable from its own pad.
Compressor
An effect that turns down the loudest moments so the overall sound is steadier and fuller.
Count-in
A bar of clicks before recording starts, so you can find the beat.
Cutoff
The point where a filter starts removing sound.
Decay
How a sound fades away after it starts. Release is similar, for sounds that play only while a pad is held.
Envelope
A shape that changes something, usually volume or filter, over the life of a sound.
Filter
Removes some frequencies. Low-pass makes sound darker; high-pass makes it thinner.
Home note
The note a piece of music feels most at rest on. Also called the root.
Kit
The set of sounds on the pads of a project.
Knob FX
One effect, controlled by K1 to K3, applied to the pads you choose.
Layering
Playing two or more sounds together to make a richer one.
Loop
A short piece of music that repeats. Also the LOOP button, which repeats a single sound.
Metronome
A click that marks the beat while you record.
Mono / Poly
Mono: a new hit stops the last. Poly: hits overlap and ring together.
Normalize
Raising a sound so its loudest point reaches full level.
Note Repeat
Makes a held pad repeat steadily at a chosen note length.
Octave
Twelve semitones. The same note, higher or lower.
Overdub
Recording new parts on top of what’s already recorded.
Pad bank
One of eight sets of 16 pads, lettered A to H.
Pan
Where a sound sits from left to right.
Pitch
How high or low a sound is.
Project
Everything you’re working on: sounds, sequences and song, saved together.
Quantise
Moving recorded hits to the nearest point on a timing grid. The grid spacing is called Q.
Resample
Recording what a sequence plays onto a new pad, as one new sound.
Resonance
A ringing emphasis at a filter’s cutoff point.
Reverb
An effect that imitates the echoes of a room or hall.
Sample
A recorded sound, ready to play from a pad.
Section
A part of a track, such as the intro, main section or breakdown.
Semitone
The smallest step between notes in Western music; one piano key.
Sequence
A recording of when pads were played, which loops.
Slice
One piece of a chopped recording.
Song
On the MPC Sample, a list of sequences played in order.
Swing
Delaying every other small note slightly, for a looser, rolling feel.
Tempo
The speed of the music, in BPM.
Threshold
A level that triggers something: recording, or where chop slices fall.
Velocity
How hard a pad is hit, which usually sets how loud the sound plays.
Warp
Stretching a sound longer or shorter, with or without changing its pitch.
Waveform
A picture of a sound over time. Its height shows loudness; for regular, pitched sounds, how tightly its waves repeat shows pitch.

Where Next

You made a track.
Now make another, and listen for what you’d do differently.

The fastest way to grow from here isn’t learning more buttons. It’s making lots of small things. A few ideas for future evenings:

  • One loop a night. Ten minutes, one new project, one loop. Don’t judge it; save it and move on. After a month you’ll have a sketchbook of ideas.
  • Copy a groove you love. Pick a favourite atmospheric track and try to recreate just its drum pattern. You’ll learn more about rhythm than any book can teach.
  • A sound walk. Take the MPC around the house, or out into the garden at dusk, and record ten sounds. Build a track from nothing else.
  • Explore the corners. Flex Beat ([[SHIFT]] + [[PAD FX]]), Step Edit ([[SHIFT]] + [[SEQ]]), and the fader’s other jobs ([[SHIFT]] + [[#9]]) are all waiting.
  • Learn a scale. Copy one sound to seven pads and set their Semi Tune to 0, +2, +3, +5, +7, +8 and +10: the notes of a minor scale, a ready-made palette for melodies.
  • Watch and read. Akai’s MPC Sample videos on their YouTube channel and the full online User Guide go deeper on everything here.
