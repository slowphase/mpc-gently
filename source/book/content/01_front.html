Welcome

This is a small book for evenings. It assumes you know nothing about samplers, sequencers or music theory, and it doesn’t mind. You’ll need your MPC Sample and a pair of headphones or monitors. Most lessons’ guided parts take 15 to 25 minutes; Lesson 7, the biggest, takes about half an hour. An optional Go Further challenge adds another 10 to 20 if you’d like to stay a while.

Lesson 1 is a gentle exploration of the MPC and its demo. Then, across Lessons 2 to 8, you’ll build one original piece of slow, atmospheric electronic music, a little at a time: a heartbeat of drums, a haze of sound around it, a bassline underneath, a few sounds you shape yourself, a recording of something on your kitchen table, and finally an arrangement that goes somewhere. By the last page you’ll have a finished track of about eighty seconds that is entirely yours.

Two routesGuided, or your own way

Every lesson saves a project, such as GENTLY2, and the next lesson starts from it: the guided route. Go Further then saves a second copy with a B, such as GENTLY2B, and you can start the next lesson from that instead: the creative route. Both work for every later lesson, and the guided copy is always saved first. A few challenges change your main loop or shared sounds; they say so, and later lessons allow for it.

The aim isn’t to memorise the MPC but to make music with it, so features appear only when the music needs them.

Reading the stepsHow controls are written

[[SEQ RECORD]]a button
[[@K1]]a knob (K1, K2, K3)
[[#4]]a pad, by its number
[[#A5]]pad 5 in bank A
[[~Save Project]]words on the screen
[[SHIFT]] + [[#16]]hold the first, press the second

The words printed underneath a button or pad on the MPC are its second job. You reach them by holding [[SHIFT]]. So “[[SHIFT]] + [[#16]] (PROJECT)” means: hold SHIFT, press pad 16, and the Project menu opens.

Listen on headphones or monitorsPlug headphones into PHONES (the speaker switches off), or connect powered monitors to AUDIO OUT on the back. Either way you’ll hear the bass the little speaker can’t reproduce. Protect your hearing: turn MAIN VOLUME right down before you plug in, then bring it up slowly.
A note on accuracyEvery button, menu and step has been checked against Akai’s official MPC Sample User Guide for firmware 1.3.0, but not every step has been confirmed on hardware yet. Where the guide is silent, the book says so and gives you a way to check by ear, or a method that works either way. If your screen ever disagrees with this book, believe the screen.

Meet the MPC Sample

Here is the whole top panel, as drawn in Akai’s User Guide. You don’t need to learn it now. Every lesson introduces only the few controls it uses, and this page is here when you want the big picture.

Around the edgesThe sockets and switches

POWER
Switches the MPC on and off. It isn’t on the top panel; look around the edges of the unit.
PHONES
A 3.5 mm socket for headphones. MAIN VOLUME sets the level. Plugging in turns the speaker off.
USB-C
Power and charging. Akai recommend a 5 V, 2 A supply, and note that some cables and chargers only charge the MPC while it’s switched off.
AUDIO OUT
6.35 mm sockets for powered monitors or other suitable audio equipment.
AUDIO IN
6.35 mm sockets for recording from external sources; the REC GAIN knob on the back sets the incoming level. Optional: this book uses the built-in microphone.
microSD slot
On the left side. Optional extra storage, and the only storage Akai describes a computer reaching. Everything in this book fits on the internal drive. See Your Sounds and Storage.

The battery runs for around five hours. Its icon beside MAIN VOLUME is green when there’s plenty left, amber below about 29 percent, and red when it’s time to charge.

The big ideaSounds and sequences

Almost everything on the MPC is one of two things.

A sample is a recorded sound sitting on a pad: a drum, a note, a voice, a mug being tapped with a spoon. There are 16 pads and 8 pad banks, lettered A to H, so one project can hold 128 sounds. The lit letter beside MAIN VOLUME tells you which bank you’re on; [[PAD BANK]] moves to the next one.

A sequence is a recording of when you hit the pads. Play it back and the MPC plays the pads for you, looping round and round. A project can hold up to 128 sequences. One sequence plays at a time, and a song is simply a list of sequences played in order.

All of it, sounds and sequences together, lives in a project.

The mode buttonsWhat the pads do depends on the mode

[[SAMPLE]]
Pads play sounds. The screen shows the last sound you played and lets you change it.
[[SEQ]]
Pads choose and launch sequences. Pads with something recorded glow green; empty ones are dim.
[[PAD FX]]
Pads become effects you play over the whole mix while a sequence runs.
[[KNOB FX]]
Hands K1, K2 and K3 to one effect. Which effect, and which pads it affects, is chosen in FX SELECT ([[SHIFT]] + [[KNOB FX]]).
The most common surpriseIf you press a pad expecting a drum and a sequence starts instead, you’re in Sequence Mode. Press [[SAMPLE]] and the pads play sounds again.

The screenButtons above, knobs below

The three buttons above the screen, [[B1]] [[B2]] [[B3]], and the three knobs below the panel line, [[@K1]] [[@K2]] [[@K3]], change their jobs from page to page. The screen always tells you what they do right now: labels along the top are for the buttons, labels along the bottom are for the knobs.

The big ENCODER wheel scrolls through lists and values; pressing it chooses. [[−]] and [[+]] nudge values down and up. Two shortcuts worth knowing from day one: [[SHIFT]] + [[−]] is Undo, and [[SHIFT]] + [[+]] is Redo.

If a knob seems to do nothingK1 to K3 are ordinary knobs with a start and an end, not endless wheels. If the value on screen is far from where the knob is physically pointing, the MPC may wait for the knob to “catch up” before it responds. Keep turning towards the arrow shown on screen and it will take over. (Firmware 1.3 made the knobs follow more smoothly by default; the fader still catches up this way.)
