Lesson
06

Everything Is an Instrument

Record a sound from your table, then trim it, chop it and play it.

TimeAbout 25 minutes, plus Go Further
You’ll needA mug and a teaspoon, and a quiet room
OutcomeYour own recording in the loop, GENTLY6

The IdeaSampling the world

This is what the MPC was born to do. Sampling means recording any sound at all and turning it into an instrument. A teaspoon on a mug, keys on a table, a kettle clicking off, a breath, a page turning: once a sound is on a pad, you can tune it, shape it and play it in time like any drum.

Two habits make recordings good. First, level: close enough to the microphone to be clear, not so close that loud taps distort. Second, space: leave a little silence between sounds so they’re easy to separate later.

Separating them is called chopping. The MPC can find the start of each sound automatically and put each piece, or slice, on its own pad.

Meet the ControlsRecording and chopping

[[SAMPLE RECORD]]
Opens the recording page. Tap an empty pad to start recording onto it, and tap it again to stop.
[[@K1]] Source
Where sound comes from. Mic is the built-in microphone.
[[@K2]] Length
Free records for as long as you like.
[[@K3]] Threshold
How loud a sound must be to start the recording. Lowest means “start straight away”.
[[B1]] Monitor
Whether you hear the source while recording: Off, Auto or On.
[[CHOP]]
Slices the selected sound. Press again to return to normal.
[[SHIFT]] + [[B1]]
In Chop Mode, Extract: copies the selected slice to a new pad of its own.
[[SHIFT]] + [[#13]]
TRIM SAMPLE: permanently cuts away everything outside Start and End.

Where sound can come from

Tonight uses the built-in microphone, so you need nothing else. The Source setting offers other routes for later, all optional:

  • Mic: the built-in microphone beside the ENCODER.
  • Rear, Rear L, Rear R: the AUDIO IN sockets on the back, for a microphone, instrument or other equipment. The REC GAIN knob on the back sets their level.
  • Resample: the MPC’s own sound, re-recorded.
  • USB, USB L, USB R: audio from a connected computer.

Level, listening and keeping it

Watch the meters beside the screen as you record. If they light red, the sound is too loud and may distort: for the microphone, move the sound further away or play it more softly. Monitor decides whether you hear the source while recording: Off, Auto (only while the recording page is showing) or On. A recording lives in your project, so saving the project keeps it. Save Sample (Lesson 5) is only for using it in other projects too.

Let’s Make SomethingA mug becomes a drum

Before you startGENTLY5 (or GENTLY5B) is loaded. Before editing sample settings, check KNOB FX. If it is lit, press it to disengage it. [[SEQ RECORD]] isn’t flashing.

AGet ready

  1. Find a mug and a teaspoon, somewhere quiet. Turn MAIN VOLUME down a little before you start.
  2. Press [[SAMPLE]], then [[PAD BANK]] until D is lit.
  3. Press [[SAMPLE RECORD]]. Turn [[@K1]] (Source) to Mic.
  4. Set [[@K2]] to Free (no fixed length), and turn [[@K3]] (Threshold) all the way down so recording begins the moment you tap the pad.
  5. Press [[B1]] until Monitor reads Auto. Tap the mug: you’ll hear it through your headphones. (The speaker switches itself off while the mic is in use. On monitors, keep the volume low, or use headphones, so the mic doesn’t pick them up.)

BRecord

  1. Hold the mug about a hand’s width or two from the microphone grille beside the ENCODER.
  2. Tap [[#D1]] to start recording. Tap the mug five times, about a second apart, in different places: rim, side, handle, base, rim. Then tap [[#D1]] again to stop.
  3. Press [[SAMPLE]] and tap [[#D1]] to hear your recording.
While recording, touch only pad D1Tapping other pads during a recording drops chop markers into it. Harmless, but confusing. If it happens, just record again: tap [[#D1]], press [[SAMPLE RECORD]] and start from step 6.

CTrim and level

  1. Press [[B1]] until Trim appears. Turn [[@K1]] (Start) to just before the first tap and [[@K2]] (End) to just after the last. Hold [[SHIFT]] while turning to zoom in.
  2. Hold [[SHIFT]] and press [[B2]] (Normalize) to bring the level up.
  3. Hold [[SHIFT]] and press [[#13]] (TRIM SAMPLE). The silence you skipped is cut away for good, which keeps things tidy for chopping.

DChop

  1. Press [[CHOP]]. The recording is sliced, and pads 1, 2, 3… now play the slices. Check that [[@K3]] (Chop Type) shows Threshold; turn it if not.
  2. Tap the pads. Ideally each plays one tap of the mug, but the count won’t always be exactly five. If there are too many slices, hold [[SHIFT]] and turn [[@K3]] (Threshold) up; too few, turn it down.
  3. Pick your favourite slice, perhaps a deep one. Tap its pad, then hold [[SHIFT]] and press [[B1]] (Extract). It’s copied to the next free pad as a sound of its own.
  4. Pick a second, brighter slice and extract it the same way.
  5. Press [[CHOP]] to leave Chop Mode. Pad D1 plays the whole recording again, and your two extracted sounds sit on the next free pads, most likely [[#D2]] and [[#D3]]. Tap to check. If they landed elsewhere, use those pads wherever this book says D2 and D3.

EMake it musical

  1. Tap [[#D2]]. Press [[B2]] until Tune appears and set Semi Tune to −12: a low, woody “tock”. Press [[B1]] until Amp Env appears and shorten the Decay a little with [[@K2]].
  2. Tap [[#D3]]. Press [[B1]] until Mix appears, and turn [[@K3]] (Pan) a little to the left so it sits to one side.
  3. Press [[SEQ]], press [[PAD BANK]] until A is lit, and tap [[#1]].
  4. Press [[SEQ RECORD]], then [[PAD BANK]] until D is lit. Press [[PLAY]]. After the count-in, tap [[#D2]] on the “and” after two, and [[#D3]] on the “and” after four. Quantising tidies them.
  5. Press [[SEQ RECORD]], listen, then [[STOP]]. (Not right? [[SHIFT]] + [[−]] and try again.) Save as GENTLY6.

Go FurtherA household audition

About 15 to 20 minutes. Record three more sounds and choose the best.

  • Collect. Record three different sounds onto [[#D5]], [[#D6]] and [[#D7]]: keys jingling, a page turning, a kettle clicking off, a breath, a hum. Watch the meters; keep them out of the red.
  • Tidy each one with Start, End and Normalize, as in steps 9 and 10.
  • Audition. Press [[PLAY]] and, without recording, play each sound over your loop. Which sits best? Try tuning it down, or chopping it and extracting one slice.
  • Use the winner. Record it into sequence 1 as in steps 19 to 21. This changes your main loop; Lesson 7 reminds you to erase it from the quieter sections. Save as GENTLY6B.

What You’ve Learned

  • Sampling turns any sound into an instrument.
  • [[SAMPLE RECORD]]: choose a source, tap a pad to start and stop.
  • Chop slices a recording; Extract gives a slice its own pad.
  • Keep the meters out of the red; saving the project keeps your recordings.

Your CreationGENTLY6

Your loop, now with two sounds that exist nowhere else in the world: your mug, tuned and placed in the groove.

Lesson
07

Give It Somewhere to Go

From one loop to a journey: contrast, variation and transitions.

TimeAbout 25 to 30 minutes, plus Go Further
Starts fromGENTLY6 or GENTLY6B
OutcomeConnected sections of your track, GENTLY7

The IdeaA loop is a room; a track is a walk

Play one loop for ninety seconds and it becomes wallpaper. A track holds attention because it changes: it starts small, grows, pulls back, and returns. The changes don’t need to be big. Mostly they come from taking things away.

  • Contrast. A section with fewer parts makes the full section feel bigger when it returns.
  • Variation. The same idea, slightly altered, keeps things familiar but alive.
  • Transitions. A short “lift”, such as a snare roll, tells the listener a change is coming.

Each part of a track is called a section. Common ones are the intro (a gentle beginning), the main section (everything playing), a breakdown (things stripped back), and an outro (fading away). On the MPC Sample, each section is simply its own sequence. You’ll make them by copying your loop and taking parts out.

Meet the ControlsCopying and carving

[[SHIFT]] + [[#3]]
DOUBLE SEQ: doubles the length of the selected sequence and repeats what’s in it.
[[SHIFT]] + [[ERASE]] (COPY)
In Sequence Mode, copies the selected sequence to the pads you tap.
[[ERASE]] + pad
With [[SEQ RECORD]] flashing, removes every hit of that pad from the selected sequence. The sound stays on the pad.
[[NOTE REPEAT]] + [[B1]] to [[B3]]
Repeats a held pad; the B buttons choose how fast.
[[STOP]]
With [[SEQ RECORD]] flashing and nothing playing, disarms recording. If it keeps flashing, press [[SEQ RECORD]] once.

Same sounds, different notes

Three layers are worth keeping apart in your head. The samples on the pads are shared by the whole project. Each sequence is its own record of which pads play, and when. The arrangement is the order the sequences play in. So all five of your sections use the same kick; erasing the kick from the Intro removes those hits from that one sequence, and the kick sound stays on pad A1 for every other section. Change the kick sound itself, and it changes everywhere.

Let’s Make SomethingFive sections from one loop

Before you startGENTLY6 (or GENTLY6B) is loaded. [[KNOB FX]], [[16 LEVELS]] and [[NOTE REPEAT]] are dark, and [[SEQ RECORD]] isn’t flashing.

AGive the loop room to breathe

  1. Press [[SEQ]], press [[PAD BANK]] until A is lit, and tap [[#1]].
  2. Hold [[SHIFT]] and press [[#3]] (DOUBLE SEQ). Sequence 1 is now four bars long: your two bars, played twice. Press [[PLAY]] to check, then [[STOP]].

BMake four copies

  1. Still in Sequence Mode with bank A lit, check pads [[#2]] to [[#5]] are dim (empty). On this book’s route they always are; if one is lit, stop and load your saved copy again. Tap [[#1]] to select it, hold [[SHIFT]] + [[ERASE]] (COPY), tap [[#2]], [[#3]], [[#4]] and [[#5]] so they turn red, then let go. You now have five identical sequences.
  2. Give them jobs. Pencil this in if it helps: 1 Main, 2 Intro, 3 Break, 4 Outro, 5 Lift.

CCarve each section

Same routine every time, from the top: select ([[SEQ]], light bank A, tap the sequence), arm ([[SEQ RECORD]] flashing, no PLAY), erase ([[PAD BANK]] to the right bank, hold [[ERASE]], tap pads), disarm ([[STOP]]; if [[SEQ RECORD]] still flashes, press it once). Erasing removes those pads’ hits from the selected sequence only; the sounds stay on their pads. If a part still plays afterwards, select that sequence again and check every pad it uses.

  1. Intro, sequence 2. Press [[SEQ]], light bank A, tap [[#2]]. Press [[SEQ RECORD]]. With bank A lit, hold [[ERASE]] and tap [[#A1]] [[#A2]]. Press [[PAD BANK]] until B is lit, hold [[ERASE]] and tap [[#B1]] [[#B2]] [[#B3]]. Press [[PAD BANK]] until D is lit, hold [[ERASE]] and tap your mug pads ([[#D2]] [[#D3]]). Press [[STOP]]. Hats and haze remain; [[PLAY]] to listen, then [[STOP]].
  2. Break, sequence 3. Press [[SEQ]], light bank A, tap [[#3]]. Press [[SEQ RECORD]]. With bank A lit, hold [[ERASE]] and tap [[#A1]] [[#A2]] [[#A3]]. Press [[STOP]]. Haze, bass and mugs remain.
  3. Outro, sequence 4. Press [[SEQ]], light bank A, tap [[#4]]. Press [[SEQ RECORD]]. Erase [[#A1]] [[#A2]] [[#A3]] in bank A, [[#B1]] [[#B2]] [[#B3]] in bank B, and the mug pads in bank D. Press [[STOP]]. Only the haze remains.
  4. Lift, sequence 5. Press [[SEQ]], light bank A, tap [[#5]]. Press [[SEQ RECORD]]. With bank A lit, hold [[ERASE]] and tap only [[#A1]]. Press [[STOP]].
Two things to watchIf you continued from a B version and added your own sounds to sequence 1 (Lesson 6’s Go Further does), erase those pads from the Intro and Outro too. And only erase with [[SEQ RECORD]] flashing: with it dark, [[SAMPLE]] lit and nothing playing, [[ERASE]] and a pad removes the sound from the pad instead.
Optional: your swell in the BreakTo bring Lesson 5’s reversed swell into your track: press [[SEQ]], light bank A, and tap [[#3]] (Break). Press [[SEQ RECORD]], then [[PAD BANK]] until C is lit. Press [[PLAY]], and after the count-in tap [[#C3]] once on the one of bar 1 and once on the one of bar 3. Press [[SEQ RECORD]], then [[STOP]]. Not right? [[SHIFT]] + [[−]] (UNDO) and try again, or skip it: the track works without it.

DAdd a snare roll to the Lift

  1. Select the Lift again, even if you just did: press [[SEQ]], light bank A, and tap [[#5]]. Press [[SEQ RECORD]] and check bank A is lit. Press [[NOTE REPEAT]] and choose 1/16 (or the nearest) with the B button shown on screen.
  2. Press [[PLAY]]. Count the bars: “one two three four, two two three four, three two three four, four two three four”. On the first beat of bar four, hold [[#A2]] down until the end of the bar. A quick, steady rattle leads into whatever comes next.
  3. Press [[SEQ RECORD]], then [[STOP]], and [[NOTE REPEAT]] to switch it off. Listen to sequence 5. Not right? [[SHIFT]] + [[−]] (UNDO) and repeat steps 9 to 11.

EPlay your arrangement live

Write the order on paper first: Intro, Main, Main, Break, Lift, Main, Outro, which is sequences 2, 1, 1, 3, 5, 1, 4. Seeing an arrangement written down makes it easier to change later. Lesson 8 shows it as a picture.

  1. Press [[SEQ]], light bank A, tap [[#2]] (Intro) and press [[PLAY]]. While it plays, tap [[#1]] to queue Main. Main loops by itself: once it starts its second time round, queue [[#3]] (Break), then [[#5]] (Lift), then [[#1]], and finally [[#4]] (Outro). Press [[STOP]] when the Outro ends.
  2. Save as GENTLY7.

Go FurtherTension and release

About 15 to 20 minutes. Tension is the feeling that something is about to happen; release is when it does.

  • Make a “Hold” section in sequence 16, the slot kept free for it. In Sequence Mode with bank A lit, check [[#16]] is dim; if not, use any dim pad from 9 to 16. Copy sequence 1 to it, select it, arm, and erase what keeps it grounded, perhaps the kick and the bass. Disarm with [[STOP]].
  • Try three orders that include it, live. Where does Main feel biggest: after Hold, or after the Lift?
  • Drop things out live. While Main plays, press [[SAMPLE]], then [[MUTE]], and tap the hats for a bar of silence. Mutes delete nothing; [[SHIFT]] + [[MUTE]] (UNMUTE ALL) when you finish.
  • Colour the Lift. While the Lift plays, press [[PAD FX]] and hold [[#6]] (LP Filter) or [[#16]] (Reverb). Press [[SEQ]] to return.

Save as GENTLY7B, and keep your favourite order on paper for Lesson 8’s Go Further.

What You’ve Learned

  • Tracks move through sections with different energy.
  • Taking parts away creates contrast.
  • DOUBLE SEQ, COPY and ERASE turn one loop into many.
  • Samples are shared; each sequence has its own hits; the arrangement is the order.

Lesson
08

Your First Track

Balance it, arrange it in Song Mode, and export it as audio.

TimeAbout 20 to 25 minutes, plus Go Further
Starts fromGENTLY7 or GENTLY7B
OutcomeA finished track of about 80 seconds, GENTLY8

The IdeaFinishing

Two jobs turn your sections into a finished piece.

Mixing is setting how loud each part is, and where it sits from left to right, so everything can be heard and nothing fights. Headphones are good for this, because you hear detail clearly; if you have monitors, check the balance on them too. If you like, a gentle compressor on the whole mix can even out the loudest moments; it’s optional.

Song Mode is a playlist of sequences. You list them in order, press PLAY, and the MPC plays the whole track for you, the same way every time. The last step is to export it: the MPC records the song into a single audio file, a finished piece of music rather than a set of instructions for the MPC.

How long will it be? Each of your sequences is four bars of four beats: 16 beats. At 84 BPM a beat lasts about 0.7 seconds, so each section lasts about 11 seconds. Seven sections make roughly 80 seconds of music.

Meet the ControlsMix, compressor and song

Mix page
[[@K1]] Volume and [[@K3]] Pan for the selected pad. [[SHIFT]] + [[@K2]] is Kit Volume, the level of everything.
[[SHIFT]] + [[#5]]
COMPRESSOR. [[B3]] on/off, [[@K1]] Attack, [[@K2]] Release, [[@K3]] Amount, [[B1]] Color.
[[SHIFT]] + [[#12]]
SONG. Pads choose a sequence, [[B3]] inserts it, [[B2]] deletes the highlighted one, the ENCODER scrolls the list, [[B1]] opens export options.
[[SHIFT]] + [[PLAY]]
CONTINUE: in Song Mode, plays from the highlighted section onwards.
[[KNOB FX]]
Keep it dark while you mix, so K1 to K3 reach the Mix page. It hands the knobs to the effect; the reverb itself is set up in FX SELECT.

Let’s Make SomethingThe finished piece

Before you startGENTLY7 (or GENTLY7B) is loaded. Before editing sample settings, check KNOB FX. If it is lit, press it to disengage it.

ABalance the mix

  1. Press [[SEQ]], press [[PAD BANK]] until A is lit, tap [[#1]] (Main) and press [[PLAY]]. Press [[SAMPLE]]: the loop keeps playing while you work.
  2. Tap each part’s pad (use [[PAD BANK]] to reach banks B and D), press [[B1]] until Mix appears, and set Volume with [[@K1]], in this order: kick, bass, snare, hats, haze, mugs. A good starting balance:
    • Kick and bass are the strongest pair, about equal.
    • Snare a little below them. Hats noticeably quieter.
    • Haze behind everything, like weather. Mugs just audible.
  3. Use [[@K3]] (Pan) to give things their own place: hats a little right, the mugs a little left. Keep kick, snare and bass in the centre.
  4. Watch the meters beside the screen. If they hit the top constantly, hold [[SHIFT]] and turn [[@K2]] (Kit Volume) down a little.
  5. Optional. A compressor evens out the loudest moments. A finished track doesn’t need one, and delicate atmospheric music often sounds best without, so treat this as a finishing touch to try. Hold [[SHIFT]] and press [[#5]] (COMPRESSOR). Press [[B3]] to switch it on. Set [[@K3]] (Amount) to about 25%, [[@K1]] (Attack) to about 20 ms, and [[@K2]] (Release) to about 100 ms. Press [[B1]] to add Color, a touch of tape-style warmth.
  6. Press [[B3]] a few times to hear the mix with and without the compressor. It should sound slightly fuller and steadier, not squashed. Keep whichever you prefer, and press [[STOP]].

BBuild the song

  1. Press [[SEQ]] and check bank A is lit. Hold [[SHIFT]] and press [[#12]] (SONG). The Song page opens, empty.
  2. Tap [[#2]] (Intro) and press [[B3]]. It appears as the first step of the song.
  3. Add the rest in this order: [[#1]], [[#1]], [[#3]], [[#5]], [[#1]], [[#4]]. Each time: make sure the last step in the list is highlighted (turn the ENCODER if needed), tap the pad, press [[B3]]. New steps go after the highlighted one.
  4. Check the list reads 2, 1, 1, 3, 5, 1, 4. To remove a wrong step, highlight it and press [[B2]].
  5. Press [[PLAY]]. Sit back, close your eyes, and listen to your track from beginning to end. Press [[STOP]] when it finishes. Haze sounding dry? Open FX SELECT ([[SHIFT]] + [[KNOB FX]]) and check pads 5 and 6 are bright and the reverb isn’t bypassed with [[B3]]. Akai’s guide doesn’t say whether the reverb needs [[KNOB FX]] lit to be heard, so if it’s still dry, light [[KNOB FX]] and play again.

CKeep it safe

  1. Still on the Song page, press [[B1]] for export options, then [[B3]] to export the song as a new sequence. The whole track is placed in the next free sequence as one long sequence: a backup of your arrangement that can’t be shuffled by accident.
  2. Save as GENTLY8.

DExport your track

  1. Hold [[SHIFT]] and press [[#12]] (SONG) to open the Song page again. Press [[B1]] for export options, then [[B2]] (audio mixdown).
  2. Name it GENTLY8 with the ENCODER, as you name projects, then press [[B3]] (Do It!). Wait while the MPC records the song into an audio file. That file is your finished track.
Where the file goesThree separate things, and only the first is certain.
  1. Exporting. Akai documents the audio mixdown, so once it finishes, your track exists as an audio file on the MPC. That’s this lesson’s outcome.
  2. Where it’s stored. Akai’s guide doesn’t say which drive or folder the file goes to, and having a microSD card in the slot doesn’t guarantee it lands there.
  3. Copying it to a computer (optional). The guide describes computer access only for the microSD card, through SD Card Access: see Your Sounds and Storage. If the file isn’t on the card, this book can’t yet tell you how to reach it; that’s still to be tested on hardware.
Tempo jumping between sections?You set GBL in Lesson 2 so every sequence shares one tempo. If sections still change speed, press [[SEQ]], press [[B2]] until GBL shows, and set 84 with [[B1]] and the ENCODER.

Go FurtherYour own version

About 20 minutes, and entirely your decisions. Make a second version of the track, save it as GENTLY8B and export it too. GENTLY8 stays safe.

  • Rearrange. Build a different order in Song Mode, perhaps the favourite you wrote down in Lesson 7, or one that uses your sketchpad variations (sequences 9 to 14, and 16).
  • Fade the ending. The MPC can record knob moves into a sequence. Press [[SEQ]], light bank A and tap [[#4]] (Outro). Press [[SEQ RECORD]], then [[SAMPLE]] (recording stays armed). Hold [[SAMPLE]] and press [[#A5]]: this selects the haze without playing or recording it. With [[KNOB FX]] dark, press [[B1]] until Mix appears. Press [[PLAY]], and over the four bars turn [[@K1]] (Volume) slowly down until the haze is nearly silent. Press [[SEQ RECORD]], then [[STOP]], and listen. To undo straight away, [[SHIFT]] + [[−]]. To remove it later, select the haze the same way, hold [[ERASE]] and turn [[@K1]], then press [[B3]] to confirm. Then play sequence 1: if the haze has stayed quiet there too, undo or remove the fade and leave this one for now.
  • Perform over it. While the song plays, press [[PAD FX]] and hold a pad during the Break; [[#6]] LP Filter and [[#16]] Reverb are lovely here.
  • Compare. Listen to both versions back to back. What did you change, and why? That question is how every producer learns.

What You’ve Learned

  • Mixing: volume and pan for each part.
  • A compressor is an optional finishing touch, not a requirement.
  • Song Mode plays sequences in a fixed order.
  • Export a song as a sequence for safekeeping, and as audio to share.
  • You can make a track from nothing, standalone.

Your CreationGENTLY8

A complete original track, about 80 seconds long, with an intro, a main section, a breakdown, a lift and an outro, built entirely on the MPC Sample and exported as an audio file. Well done.
