Lesson
03

Into the Atmosphere

Slow it down, soften it, and give it a room to ring in.

TimeAbout 15 to 20 minutes, plus Go Further
Starts fromGENTLY2 or GENTLY2B
OutcomeA slow, spacious, layered loop, GENTLY3

The IdeaSounds that hang in the air

Drums hit. Atmospheric sounds hang: they swell in gently, ring on, and fade away slowly, filling the space between the drums like mist. The right starting sound helps a lot, and then three simple moves do the rest:

  • Lower it. Dropping the pitch makes a sound slower, darker and bigger. Twelve steps down is called an octave: the same note, but deeper.
  • Soften it. A slow fade-in removes the hard edge at the start. A filter takes away the bright top, like hearing music through a wall.
  • Give it a room. Reverb imitates the echoes of a space. A large reverb makes a sound seem far away and enormous.

Finally, layering: two versions of a sound played together, one low and one high, blend into something richer than either.

Length, overlap and how a pad plays

A drum hit lasts a fraction of a second; a good atmospheric sound lasts several. That length is what lets one tap fill a whole bar, and it’s why a slow fade-in works on a long sound but would swallow a short one.

Two settings decide what happens when sounds overlap or when you let go of a pad:

  • One Shot or Note On. In One Shot (the normal setting) a tap plays the whole sound, however briefly you touch the pad. In Note On the sound plays only while you hold the pad, and stops when you let go. A small note icon on the Sample page shows Note On is active. Tonight uses One Shot.
  • Mono or Poly. With Poly, a new tap lets the previous one keep ringing, so sounds overlap into a haze. With Mono, each tap cuts off the last.

Let’s Make SomethingA haze around the heartbeat

Before you startGENTLY2 or GENTLY2B is loaded (if not: [[SHIFT]] + [[#16]], [[~Load Project]], [[~User]]). [[SEQ RECORD]] isn’t flashing, and [[NOTE REPEAT]] and [[KNOB FX]] are dark.

AFind a sound that rings

  1. Press [[SAMPLE]], make sure bank A is lit, and tap [[#A5]]. Press [[SAMPLE SELECT]].
  2. Browse for a good starting sound. Folder names will guide you, and the best candidates have:
    • Length: a second or more of sound, not a quick hit.
    • A steady tone or a long, natural fade: a held synth note, a chord, keys, a pad, a voice.
    • A clear note: pitched sounds still sound musical an octave down.
    • Enough body that something remains after a slow fade-in removes the start.
  3. Load it with the ENCODER and close the browser with [[SAMPLE SELECT]].
Can’t find anything sustained?Use a crash or ride cymbal from the drum folders. It rings for several seconds, and lowered and filtered it becomes a soft, airy wash. It won’t be a lush synth pad, and that’s fine. Avoid short drum hits: a slow fade-in would leave almost nothing to hear.

BLower it and soften it

  1. Press [[B2]] until Tune appears. Turn [[@K1]] (Semi Tune) to −12, one octave down. Tap [[#A5]]: slower, darker, bigger.
  2. Press [[B1]] until Amp Env appears. Turn [[@K1]] (Attack) to about 80. Tap the pad: it now swells in instead of starting abruptly.
  3. Still on Amp Env, turn [[@K2]] (Decay) while tapping the pad, and find the setting that lets the sound ring on longest.
  4. Press [[B3]] until Filter appears. Turn [[@K3]] (Type) to LPF2. Now turn [[@K1]] (Cutoff) slowly down while tapping: the sound grows darker and further away. Stop when it’s soft but still clear, somewhere around 60. Add a touch of [[@K2]] (Reso), about 20, for a gentle glow.
  5. Press [[B2]] until Play appears. Set [[@K1]] (Polyphony) to Poly, so each new tap lets the previous one keep ringing. If the note icon is showing, press [[SHIFT]] + [[CHOP]] (NOTE ON) to switch back to One Shot.

CLayer it

  1. Copy the pad: with [[#A5]] selected, hold [[SHIFT]] and press [[ERASE]] (COPY). Keep holding both, and tap [[#A6]] so it turns red. Let go. Pad A6 now has a copy of the sound.
  2. Tap [[#A6]]. Press [[B2]] until Tune appears and set [[@K1]] to 0, an octave above pad 5. Press [[B1]] until Mix appears and turn [[@K1]] (Volume) down to about −8 dB, so it sits quietly on top.
  3. Tap [[#A5]], press [[B2]] until Play appears, then hold [[SHIFT]] and turn [[@K2]] (Pad Link) to 6. Now one tap on pad 5 plays both pads together.

DGive it a room

Knob FX involves three separate things: choosing the effect and its pads (FX SELECT), adjusting it (with [[KNOB FX]] lit, K1 to K3 belong to the effect), and bypassing it ([[B3]] in FX SELECT), which you won’t need tonight.

  1. Open FX SELECT. Hold [[SHIFT]] and press [[KNOB FX]]. The Knob FX settings appear.
  2. Choose the effect. Turn the ENCODER to [[~Reverb Large]] and press the ENCODER to load it.
  3. Choose the pads. Tap [[#5]] and [[#6]] (bank A) so they’re bright. Bright pads get the reverb; dim pads stay dry. (Don’t press [[B2]] here: that applies the effect to all pads.)
  4. Engage and adjust. Press [[KNOB FX]] so it lights, then press [[SAMPLE]] and tap [[#A5]]. K1 to K3 now control the reverb: turn [[@K3]] (Mix) to about halfway and [[@K2]] (Time) well up. Leave [[@K1]] (Pre-Delay) low.
  5. Disengage. Press [[KNOB FX]] so it goes dark. K1 to K3 return to your sample settings.
Knobs changing the wrong thing?While [[KNOB FX]] is lit, K1 to K3 belong to the effect and won’t adjust your sample settings. From now on, before editing sample settings, check KNOB FX. If it is lit, press it to disengage it. Only one Knob FX can be active at a time, so this reverb is the “room” for your whole track. Disengaging hands back the knobs; switching the effect off is a separate job, the Bypass ([[B3]] in FX SELECT). Akai’s guide doesn’t say whether the reverb keeps sounding while [[KNOB FX]] is dark, so tap pad A5 and listen. If it’s gone, light [[KNOB FX]] whenever you want to hear it.

EAdd it to your loop

  1. Press [[SEQ]], press [[PAD BANK]] until A is lit, and tap [[#1]] to select your loop. Press [[SEQ RECORD]], then [[PLAY]].
  2. After the count-in, tap [[#A5]] exactly on the one of each bar. Recording adds your new hits to the drums already there. Press [[SEQ RECORD]] to stop recording and just listen, then [[STOP]].
  3. Not happy with the take? [[SHIFT]] + [[−]] (UNDO) removes everything recorded since you pressed PLAY. Try again from step 17.
  4. Save as GENTLY3.

Go FurtherTwo weathers

About 15 minutes. Make two contrasting atmospheres from your haze and play them over the loop by hand, without recording.

  • Copy. Select [[#A5]] and copy it to [[#A7]] and [[#A8]]. On each copy, set Pad Link ([[SHIFT]] + [[@K2]] on the Play page) to Off, so they play on their own.
  • A7, cold and far: higher (Semi Tune 0 or +12), a high-pass filter, a very slow attack. In FX SELECT, tap [[#7]] so it gets the reverb too.
  • A8, warm and close: lower (−24), a low cutoff, a quicker attack, no reverb. Then try Note On ([[SHIFT]] + [[CHOP]]): hold the pad for as long as you want it to sound.
  • Listen. Press [[PLAY]] and play A7 for a few bars, then A8. Which suits your heartbeat? There’s no right answer, only your choice.
  • Also try, on A7 or A8 rather than your haze: [[LOOP]] for a drone that rings until you tap it again, or [[SHIFT]] + [[LOOP]] (REVERSE) so it rises instead of fades. Or another reverb such as [[~Spring Reverb]] in FX SELECT.

The reverb is shared by the whole project, so return to [[~Reverb Large]] and disengage [[KNOB FX]], then save as GENTLY3B.

What You’ve Learned

  • An octave is 12 semitones: the same note, higher or lower.
  • Slow attack, low-pass filter and reverb make sounds atmospheric.
  • [[B1]] [[B2]] [[B3]] flip through the Sample Mode pages.
  • COPY and Pad Link make layers; Poly lets sounds overlap.
  • One Shot plays the whole sound; Note On only while held.
  • Knob FX: choose effect and pads, engage, adjust, disengage.

Lesson
04

The Low End

Notes, steps and octaves, and a bassline from a single sound.

TimeAbout 20 minutes, plus Go Further
Starts fromGENTLY3 or GENTLY3B
OutcomeDrums, bass and atmosphere together, GENTLY4

The IdeaA ladder of notes

The bass is the low part underneath everything. It gives music its weight and much of its mood. A bassline usually plays one note at a time and moves between just a few notes.

Think of musical notes as rungs on a ladder. The smallest step from one rung to the next is a semitone (one key along on a piano, black or white). Climb twelve semitones and you reach the octave, which you met last lesson: the same note again, only higher.

Most music has a home note, the one that feels like rest. A bassline wanders away from home and comes back. Different distances from home have different characters. You don’t need their names; your ears will tell you. Some useful ones:

  • 3 semitones up sounds dark and wistful, the colour of a minor key.
  • 5 up feels like a lift, leaning somewhere.
  • 7 up sounds strong and settled, almost as stable as home.
  • 2 down is a small step that wants to return home.

The MPC Sample has a lovely way to explore this called 16 Levels. It spreads one sound across all sixteen pads, each at a different pitch, turning the pads into a little keyboard for your ears. Then, to record the bassline, you’ll make your own tuned copies of the bass with Semi Tune, which Akai documents in exact semitones.

Pitch and length are different things

Changing a sound’s pitch makes it higher or lower. Changing its length makes it last longer or shorter. On a record player the two are tied together: slow the record down and the music gets lower and longer. The MPC Sample’s Warp setting lets you choose. In Time Stretch mode it changes a sound’s length but keeps its pitch; in Pitch mode it changes both, like the record. Today you’ll only change pitch, with Semi Tune. Akai’s guide doesn’t say whether Semi Tune also changes a sound’s length, so listen for that as you tune. You’ll try Warp in Lesson 5.

BDo these sounds belong together?

Your haze from Lesson 3 may have a clear note in it, and so does your bass. Notes interact: some pairs feel settled, others rub uncomfortably. You don’t need any music theory to fix this, only your ears.

  1. Press [[SEQ]], light bank A, tap [[#1]] and press [[PLAY]]. Press [[SAMPLE]], then [[PAD BANK]] until B is lit, and tap [[#B1]] on the one of each bar, along with the haze.

Lesson
05

Shape the Sound

Waveforms, envelopes, filters and effects, and one sound made into three.

TimeAbout 20 minutes, plus Go Further
Starts fromGENTLY4 or GENTLY4B
OutcomeThree new sounds from one sample, GENTLY5

The IdeaReading a sound

Sound is air wobbling back and forth. A waveform is a drawing of that wobble over time. Two things are worth reading in it: how tall the wave is (loudness) and how tightly packed its wiggles are (pitch). Pitch only shows up as a regular wiggle for sounds with a clear note, like a hum or a bass; noisy sounds such as a snare or rain don’t have a single pitch at all.

Zoom out and you see the whole sound’s life: a sudden start, then a long fade. The MPC lets you choose which part of it plays by moving its Start and End points. Nothing is thrown away; the rest is just skipped.

Envelopes: the shape of loudness over time

An envelope reshapes how loud a sound is from moment to moment. Attack is how long it takes to fade in. Decay is how it fades away. A fast attack and short decay give a plucked, percussive sound; a slow attack gives a soft swell.

Filters: choosing which part of the sound gets through

Every sound mixes low, rumbling frequencies with high, bright ones. A filter lets some through and holds others back. A low-pass filter lets the lows pass, so the sound grows darker. A high-pass filter does the opposite, making it thin and airy. The cutoff is where the change happens; resonance adds a ringing emphasis right at that point. The MPC also has a separate filter envelope, which moves the cutoff automatically each time you hit the pad.

Effects and resampling

Effects such as delay and reverb add echoes and space as the sound plays. An echo is simply the sound repeating, each time a little quieter. Resampling records what a sequence plays onto a new pad, so a whole performance becomes one new sound. Musicians call this “printing”. Today you’ll play an echo by hand and print it.

Let’s Make SomethingThree from one

Before you startGENTLY4 or GENTLY4B is loaded. Before editing sample settings, check KNOB FX. If it is lit, press it to disengage it. [[16 LEVELS]] is dark.

AChoose a source

  1. Press [[SAMPLE]], then [[PAD BANK]] until C is lit. Tap [[#C1]] and press [[SAMPLE SELECT]].
  2. Choose a sound with some life in it: a chord, a voice, a melodic hit, or a snare with a long tail. Load it. This is your original, and we won’t change it.
  3. Make two copies: with [[#C1]] selected, hold [[SHIFT]] + [[ERASE]] (COPY), tap [[#2]] and [[#3]] so both turn red, and let go.

BSound one: a pluck (pad C2)

  1. Tap [[#C2]]. Press [[B1]] until Trim appears. Turn [[@K2]] (End) down until only a short piece plays. Nudge [[@K1]] (Start) if you want to skip a slow beginning.
  2. Press [[B1]] until Amp Env appears. Set [[@K1]] (Attack) to 0. Turn [[@K2]] (Decay) to a short setting, tapping as you go, until the sound is clipped and neat.
  3. Press [[B3]] until Filter appears. Set [[@K3]] (Type) to LPF4, [[@K1]] (Cutoff) to about 40 and [[@K2]] (Reso) to about 50.
  4. Press [[B3]] until Filt Env appears. Set [[@K1]] (Attack) to 0, [[@K2]] (Decay) short, and [[@K3]] (Depth) to about 90. Tap: each hit flicks the filter open and lets it close again. That’s a pluck.

CSound two: a swell (pad C3)

  1. Tap [[#C3]]. Hold [[SHIFT]] and press [[LOOP]] (REVERSE). The sound now plays backwards and rises towards you.
  2. Press [[B1]] until Amp Env appears and set [[@K1]] (Attack) to about 100 for a slow fade-in.
  3. Press [[B2]] until Tune appears and set [[@K1]] (Semi Tune) to −5.
  4. Press [[B3]] until Filter appears. Set Type to HPF2 and Cutoff to about 40. Thinning out the lows lets it float above your bass instead of clashing with it.

DSound three: a hand-played echo, printed (pad C4)

  1. Press [[SEQ]] and press [[PAD BANK]] until A is lit. Pad [[#15]] should be dim, an empty sequence kept for this helper; if it’s lit, use any dim pad from 9 to 16 instead, wherever these steps say 15. Tap it, and set [[@K1]] (Length) to 2 bars. The tempo should read 84 (it’s shared, thanks to GBL).
  2. Press [[SEQ RECORD]], then [[PAD BANK]] until C is lit. Press [[PLAY]]. After the count-in, tap [[#C1]] on beats one, two, three and four of the first bar, each tap softer than the last: firm, medium, soft, barely there. That fading repeat is an echo. Press [[SEQ RECORD]], then [[STOP]].
  3. Press [[PLAY]] to check, then [[STOP]]. If every tap sounds equally loud, Full Level may be on ([[SHIFT]] + [[#1]] switches it off): undo with [[SHIFT]] + [[−]] and record again.
  4. With your helper sequence still selected (it’s the one you just played), hold [[SHIFT]] and press [[#11]] (RESAMPLE), then tap [[#4]], with bank C still lit. The MPC records the sequence onto pad C4. Give it a moment.
  5. Press [[SAMPLE]] and tap [[#C4]]: your four fading hits are now one sound. Press [[B1]] until Trim appears, then hold [[SHIFT]] and press [[B2]] (Normalize) to bring it up to full level.
  6. For a ghostly finish, hold [[SHIFT]] and press [[LOOP]] (REVERSE): the echoes now swell towards the sound. Press [[B2]] until Tune appears and set Semi Tune to −12.

ETidy up

  1. Delete the helper sequence so it never turns up in your song: press [[SEQ]], make sure bank A is lit, hold [[ERASE]] and tap [[#15]] (or the helper pad you used). It held only the four taps you just recorded. If the screen asks you to confirm, press [[B3]]. The pad goes dim.
  2. Save as GENTLY5.

Go FurtherOne sound, four characters

About 15 to 20 minutes. Copy C1 to [[#C5]] to [[#C8]] and turn each copy into something unrecognisable. No recipes this time: just a word for each, and the tools you know.

  • C5, tiny: a sliver of the sound. Hold [[SHIFT]] while turning Start or End on the Trim page to zoom in.
  • C6, enormous: pitch, attack and filter.
  • C7, stretched: on the Tune page, set [[@K3]] (Warp) to 200%. Press [[SHIFT]] + [[#15]] (WARP) to switch between Time Stretch (twice as long, same pitch) and Pitch (twice as long and lower). In Pitch mode Semi Tune is unavailable.
